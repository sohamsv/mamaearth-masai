### Situation
Mamaearth's e-commerce operations generated a verified cleaned total revenue of INR 97,358.30 across 175 deduplicated orders (reconciled against the raw database total of INR 99,860.20 after accounting for a INR 2,501.90 duplicate-order delta). An outlier-corrected time series analysis reveals that March (2026-03) represents the true peak revenue month at INR 20,318.90. January (2026-01) initially appeared as the top month with INR 29,582.10, but once two bulk order outliers were removed, January's baseline revenue adjusted to INR 11,637.10.

### Complication
Product returns are severely eroding operational margins, heavily concentrated in Cash on Delivery (COD) transactions. While electronic payment methods maintain healthy return rates—CARD at 14.7% and UPI at 18.9%—COD orders suffer from an alarming return rate of 44.4%. Multi-level segmentation identifies Tier-2 COD orders as the single highest-risk segment, where return rate peaks at 54.5%. Furthermore, correlation analysis shows no significant relationship between discount percentages and returns (r = -0.09, negligible), disproving the assumption that higher promotional discounts reduce return rates.

### Resolution
Regional Ops and Finance leadership must immediately execute targeted interventions:
1. Restrict or require partial digital pre-payment for COD orders in Tier-2 cities where return rates reach 54.5%.
2. Re-allocate promotional spend away from blanket discount campaigns, given their negligible impact on return mitigation.
3. Align supply chain capacity and inventory planning with the true organic demand peak observed in March (2026-03 revenue of INR 20,318.90), rather than distorted January bulk spikes.
